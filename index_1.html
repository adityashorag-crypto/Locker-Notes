Homework Help

Pick a subject. These are the tricks that actually save time — not just "study harder."

Math

Stuck on a problem? Try this order before you give up.

  • Redo it with easier numbers first. Swap the messy numbers in the problem for 1, 2, 3. If you can solve it with small numbers, you know the steps — now put the real numbers back in.
  • Say out loud what each number means. Half of stuck-ness is forgetting what you're even solving for. Name it before you touch the calculator.
  • Check your last answer by working backward. Plug your answer back into the original problem. If it doesn't fit, the mistake is usually in your last two steps, not your first.

Science

For labs, definitions, and "explain why" questions.

  • Turn vocabulary into a mini-story. "Mitochondria is the powerhouse of the cell" sticks because it's a sentence, not a word. Do that for every term you're memorizing.
  • For lab reports, write the conclusion first. Figure out what you're trying to prove, then fill in the method and data to support it — it's easier than writing in order.
  • "Why" questions want a cause-and-effect chain. Answer in the form "X happens, which causes Y, which leads to Z" — that's the structure teachers are grading for.

English

Essays, reading responses, and grammar.

  • Write your thesis last, not first. Write your three body paragraphs first, then summarize what they actually proved into one sentence. It'll be sharper than guessing your point in advance.
  • Read the last chapter's summary before the chapter. Knowing where the plot ends up makes it much easier to spot what's important while you read.
  • Read your essay out loud before submitting. Your ear catches run-on sentences and missing words that your eyes skip right over.

History

Dates, causes, and essay questions.

  • Build a timeline, not a list. Dates on their own don't stick. Draw a line, mark events on it, and connect each one with an arrow to what it caused.
  • Every event has a "because" and a "so." "The event happened because ___, so ___ happened next." Fill in both blanks for anything you need to remember.
  • For essay questions, answer the question in your first sentence. Then spend the rest of the paragraph proving that first sentence with two specific facts.

Advice Corner

The stuff nobody puts in a school handbook.

Stuck on a hard problem

Set a 10-minute timer and only work on that one thing. If you're still stuck when it rings, write down exactly where you got stuck — that's the question to ask, and it's usually a much smaller ask than "I don't get it."

The deadline feels impossible

Don't start with the hardest part. Do the easiest 20% first to get momentum, then tackle the rest — a half-full page is way less scary to keep working on than a blank one.

Nervous before a test

Write down the three things you're most likely to forget, right before you walk in. Getting them out of your head and onto paper frees up room to actually think during the test.

Fight with a friend

Wait until you're not angry to talk about it — texting mid-fight almost always makes it worse. Say what you actually felt, not what you think they did wrong.

Losing motivation

Motivation shows up after you start, not before. Commit to just five minutes of the task — most days, five minutes turns into finishing it.
