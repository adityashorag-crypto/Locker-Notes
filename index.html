📚 Homework Help

Pick a subject for quick strategies, or head to Ask Anything for something specific.

Math

Quick strategies before you Google the answer.

  • Show every step. Even if you know the answer, writing out your steps catches mistakes and earns partial credit.
  • Check with estimation. Round the numbers first — if your exact answer is way off from your estimate, recheck your work.
  • Redo it from scratch once. If you got a problem wrong, don't just find the error — solve the whole thing again fresh.
  • Learn from mistakes. Keep a short list of errors you keep repeating — reviewing it before a test catches them before they cost you points.

Science

Understand it, don't just memorize it.

  • Explain it out loud. If you can explain a concept simply to a friend, you actually understand it.
  • Draw the process. Diagrams for things like cycles or systems stick better than paragraphs.
  • Connect it to real life. Link the concept to something you've actually seen happen.
  • Use flashcards for vocab. Science is full of specific terms — quick flashcard reviews make definitions stick faster than re-reading notes.

English

Writing and reading, made less painful.

  • Outline before you write. Three bullet points for your main ideas saves you from staring at a blank page.
  • Read it out loud. Awkward sentences almost always sound wrong when spoken.
  • Cut ruthlessly. If a sentence doesn't add anything new, delete it.
  • Vary your sentence length. Mixing short and long sentences keeps writing from feeling monotonous.

History

Dates and causes, actually remembered.

  • Build a timeline. Seeing events in order makes cause-and-effect click.
  • Ask "why," not just "what." Teachers usually care more about why something happened than the exact date.
  • Turn it into a story. People remember stories better than fact lists.
  • Compare, don't just list. Compare causes and effects across events instead of listing facts — it shows deeper understanding.

📌 Advice Corkboard

A few things worth pinning up.

On failing a test

One bad grade is data, not a verdict. Figure out what went wrong, fix that one thing, and move on.

On feeling behind

Everyone's on a different timeline. Compare yourself to who you were last month, not to everyone else.

On friend drama

Give it a day before reacting. Most things feel smaller after you've slept on them.

On procrastinating

Don't aim to "finish the assignment." Aim to work on it for 10 minutes. Starting is the hard part.

On asking for help

Asking a question isn't admitting you're behind — it's how you stop being behind.

On comparing yourself to others

Someone else's highlight reel isn't their everyday life. You're only ever seeing part of the story.
